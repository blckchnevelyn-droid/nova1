# ADR-002 — Execution Attempt Identity, Idempotency, and Side-Effect Authority

## Status

ADOPTED — contract implemented at the durable execution boundary. Consequential retry/recovery remains fail-closed unless the separate Recovery Authority contract is satisfied and the execution boundary independently revalidates current authority.

## Context

ADR-001 establishes the authoritative task-state, approval-provenance, persistence, concurrency, audit, and fail-closed boundaries. It does not define how Nova identifies a consequential execution attempt or how it prevents duplicate side effects across retries, workers, crashes, and uncertain outcomes.

Nova must not infer execution outcome from worker memory, caller claims, timestamps, task state, or the absence of an error. A consequential operation can succeed externally while Nova fails to persist the result. Conversely, Nova can persist an intent without the external operation having occurred. The architecture therefore requires a durable operation boundary that distinguishes execution states without guessing.

## Decision

### 1. Operation identity
Every consequential operation has a stable **operation identity** representing the exact authorized operation to be deduplicated. It is bound to task, action/scope, and canonical operation specification. Reusing an idempotency key for materially different semantics fails closed.

### 2. Execution-attempt identity
Every execution attempt has a unique durable `attempt_id` generated by the authoritative execution boundary. Attempt identity is distinct from operation/idempotency identity.

### 3. Authoritative attempt state
Durable persistence owns attempt/side-effect state. The minimum model is `NOT_EXECUTED`, `CLAIMED`, `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED`, and `UNKNOWN`. `UNKNOWN` is first-class and must never be silently converted to another state.

### 4. Atomic claim
Claiming is an atomic authoritative persistence action. At most one active claim may hold authority for a non-idempotent operation. Fencing prevents stale workers from regaining authority.

### 5. Commit semantics
Where external side effect and persistence cannot share a transaction, crashes, timeouts, disconnects, or persistence gaps after external invocation must produce `UNKNOWN` unless independent authoritative evidence establishes another outcome.

### 6. Duplicate-effect policy
Provider idempotency should be propagated when supported. Non-idempotent operations remain blocked while `SUCCEEDED`, `UNKNOWN`, or otherwise unresolved. Recovery must reconcile or obtain an explicitly governed compensating/alternative strategy before another consequential attempt.

### 7. Retry authorization
Retry/recovery must independently revalidate current authority, task binding, scope/action, policy/version, approval validity, and operation identity. Earlier approval, worker ownership, or `UNKNOWN` is not permission.

### 8. Replay detection
Duplicate operation requests, replayed attempt IDs, stale worker commands, changed semantics under reused idempotency keys, and mismatched result submissions must be rejected or safely deduplicated by the authoritative boundary.

### 9. Concurrency model
Durable transactional concurrency control must enforce the claim invariant. A stale-writer conflict is never permission for blind replay.

### 10. Recovery interaction
Recovery must inspect authoritative attempt/side-effect state before proposing or executing retry. It must not assume `UNKNOWN` means failure, blindly replay unresolved non-idempotent effects, expand scope, bypass idempotency/claim rules, establish `SUCCEEDED` without evidence, or transition a task to `COMPLETED` without verification.

The detailed recovery authority and `UNKNOWN` reconciliation contract is maintained by `docs/architecture/RECOVERY.md` and implemented by `nova/recovery.py`.

### 11. Auditability
Consequential operation/attempt identity, authority context, approvals, ownership/fencing, initiation/outcome, `UNKNOWN`, recovery decisions, and reconciliation evidence must remain durably attributable and append-only.

### 12. Fail-closed invariant
If Nova cannot durably distinguish `NOT_EXECUTED`, `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED`, and `UNKNOWN`, consequential retry/recovery execution is blocked and the condition remains auditable for reconciliation.

## Required adversarial verification

The execution test suite covers replay, fencing, evidence integrity, terminal immutability, authority expiry, and restart survival. Recovery-specific adversarial coverage is maintained in `tests/test_recovery.py` and must pass before the recovery gate is declared fully verified.

## Implementation boundary

The implementation exposes narrow authoritative boundaries for operation identification, attempt creation, atomic claim, outcome recording, `UNKNOWN`, durable audit, and recovery-decision authorization. A recovery decision does not itself execute a retry or compensating action.

## Dependencies and gates

- ADR-001 is the prerequisite authority/state/persistence contract.
- Recovery Authority remains a mandatory gate for consequential retry/recovery.
- CI/test infrastructure must provide trustworthy execution evidence before production-grade verification is declared.

## Consequences

Execution identity and side-effect state are first-class authoritative data rather than incidental worker state. Retry is not implicit permission, uncertain outcomes are explicit, and recovery remains governed and fail-closed.
